7 flooring renovation ideas that transform a room

New flooring is the fastest way to make a room feel renovated without touching a wall. These are the seven flooring renovation ideas that change a Northern Nevada home the most — what each one actually does to a space, which rooms it suits, and roughly what it takes. Some are a whole-floor replacement; two of them are a weekend.

Start here: which change does the most?

If you only do one thing, replace a dated floor in the room you use most — that single change resets how the whole house reads, because flooring is the one surface that runs continuously through your field of view. If the floor itself is fine and you want the room to feel different, change the layout instead: the same plank laid in herringbone looks like a different house for the cost of extra labor and material.

Below, the seven ideas are ordered from biggest visual change to smallest commitment. Everything here uses flooring we actually sell and install in Carson City, so you can see it in the showroom before you commit.

The seven ideas

1. Swap a dated floor for waterproof luxury vinyl

This is the single most common renovation we do, and the one that changes a room the most for the money. Modern rigid-core luxury vinyl plank reads as real wood or stone, is 100% waterproof through the core, and shrugs off the grit, claws and spills that dulled whatever is down now. It also solves the specific problem of our climate: it does not gap or cup when the humidity swings the way solid wood does. Best for kitchens, entries, mudrooms, basements, rentals and whole-home installs.

2. Go wide-plank for a bigger, calmer room

Wider boards mean fewer seams, and fewer seams make a floor read as one continuous surface instead of a grid — which makes the room feel larger and quieter. It is a genuinely upscale look and it costs no more to install than a narrow board. In Northern Nevada, choose engineered hardwood rather than solid for wide planks: the layered core is far more dimensionally stable through our dry high-desert swings, and wide solid boards are exactly the ones that gap worst. Best for living rooms, open-plan main floors and primary bedrooms.

3. Change the layout: herringbone or chevron

Sometimes the layout, not the material, is the renovation. Laying hardwood, laminate or LVP in a herringbone or chevron pattern turns an ordinary floor into the feature of the room, at a fraction of what a designer floor costs. Be realistic about the trade-off: angled layouts take longer to set out and cut, and they generate more waste, so the labor and material both run higher than a straight lay. Worth it in an entry, a dining room, or any space you want people to notice. Ask us to price both ways on the same product — the difference is usually smaller than people expect.

4. Replace tired tile with a warmer, softer floor

Dated tile — small-format, heavy grout lines, a color that fixes the room in the year it was built — ages a house faster than almost anything else underfoot. Replacing it with waterproof LVP removes the grout entirely, warms the room up, and is far kinder underfoot and to sound. It also lets a kitchen or entry run continuously into the rooms beside it rather than stopping at a threshold strip, which is most of why the result feels bigger. Tile removal is real demolition work, so tell us it is tile when you book the estimate — it changes the prep.

5. Bring back carpet — with pattern and texture

Hard floors everywhere leave a house echoey and cold, and that is exactly what people notice after an all-LVP renovation. Carpet has come a long way from wall-to-wall beige: geometric prints, textured loop, and subtle modern stripes add warmth, personality and real sound absorption. It is also the cheapest way to make a room feel finished. Best for bedrooms, media rooms, offices, and stairs — where texture hides wear and the softness is the point.

6. Define zones with a custom area rug

The one idea here that does not involve replacing a floor. Open-plan rooms often feel unresolved because nothing tells you where the living area stops and the dining area starts — and a rug fixes that in an afternoon. We can bind custom area rugs to whatever size your space actually needs, in your choice of carpet and edge binding, including from a remnant. That means a rug that fits the room rather than the nearest standard size, layered over hardwood, LVP or laminate. Best for open-concept main floors, under dining tables, and long hallways that want a runner.

7. Make the hardest-working rooms waterproof

Less a look than a decision you stop having to think about. In kitchens, bathrooms, laundries, mudrooms and rental turns, a waterproof floor is the difference between a spill being a wipe-up and a spill being a repair. Waterproof LVP is the straightforward answer. Note the distinction that catches people out: most laminate is water-resistant, not waterproof — excellent in a dry living room, wrong next to a dishwasher. If you manage rentals, this one pays for itself; see flooring for property managers.

What a flooring renovation costs

Cost depends far more on your room than on which idea you pick: square footage, what is coming out, the condition of the subfloor, stairs, and how much furniture has to move. Tile removal and angled layouts both add labor; a straight-lay floating floor over a sound subfloor is the cheapest path. Rather than guess at a number here, we keep the installed ranges in one place — see the Carson City flooring cost guide for per-material estimates and what moves a quote up or down. Those are general industry ranges, not a Landmark quote.

If the budget is the constraint, note that financing is available — and that ideas 6 and 7 let you renovate one room at a time rather than all at once.
